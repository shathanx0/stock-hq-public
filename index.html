How the HQ Score works

0–100, same rules for every stock

A checklist score built from the HQ analysis templates: quality first, then growth, price, balance sheet, what insiders are doing, and what analysts expect. It reads the published numbers. It does not predict the share price and it does not read the news.

Insiders and politicians disclose trades days or weeks after they happen (Congress has up to 45 days), so by the time you see a trade the price may already have moved. Use this to ask better questions, not to copy trades. As the saying goes, insiders sell for many reasons but buy for one.

    About this project

    A research terminal, designed and built from scratch

    Stock Research HQ studies stocks the way an equity analyst would. It screens about 1,300 companies across the US, UK and Europe, compares any of them side by side, tracks what company insiders and members of Congress are trading, and keeps a public log of every research call with the price on the day it was made, so the track record can't be edited after the fact.

    Designed and built by Shathan AlabriConnect on LinkedIn

    How it works

    Static-first: everything is computed before you open the page
    1 · Public dataPrices, financials and analyst estimates (stockanalysis.com), SEC Form 4 insider filings (OpenInsider), congressional trade disclosures (Quiver Quantitative)
    →
    2 · Python buildersStandard library only. Rate-limit-aware fetching with backoff and caching, de-duplicates cross-listed companies, scores every stock on the same rules
    →
    3 · Static data filesAbout 150 stocks with five years of daily prices, a 1,300-stock screen, 88 politician profiles and the research log
    →
    4 · Single-page appVanilla JavaScript and Apache ECharts. No framework, no build step. Installable on a phone and works offline
    →
    5 · Cloudflare PagesDeploys on every git push. A scheduled AI agent researches the news and writes the morning brief before the London open on weekdays

    Engineering decisions

    • Static-first. The heavy work (screening, scoring, correlations) runs offline, so the live site is just files on a CDN: fast, cheap to run, and very little to attack.
    • Zero-dependency data pipeline. The Python builders use only the standard library and back off automatically when a source rate-limits.
    • One listing per company. Cross-listed stocks are matched by ISIN and kept on their home exchange, so the screen doesn't double-count.
    • Privacy by architecture. This public site is generated from a sanitised copy of the data, and an automated leak check blocks the publish if anything personal gets through. The private version sits behind an identity gateway.
    • Installable web app. A web manifest and a network-first service worker make it a home-screen app that still opens offline.

    Design decisions

    • Numbers first. IBM Plex Mono with tabular figures, so columns of prices line up digit by digit.
    • Never colour alone. Every gain or loss also carries ▲ or ▼, so it reads in greyscale and for colour-blind users.
    • A sentence under every chart. Each chart has a plain-English takeaway computed from the data, for readers who don't read charts.
    • Mobile-first. Bottom navigation under 760px, pinned first columns in wide tables, and 40px tap targets.
    • Motion with restraint. Short, purposeful transitions that switch off for reduced-motion users and pause when the tab is hidden. Light and dark themes come from one set of tokens.
